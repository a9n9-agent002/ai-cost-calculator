Why this page exists

If you make audio — a podcast, an audiobook, a course, a YouTube voice-over — your question is how many minutes do I get. Every vendor answers in characters, and the conversion is printed once in small grey text. ElevenLabs' own table says 75,000 ~75 min and 12,375,000 ~12,375 min, i.e. it divides characters by 1,000. That number is the whole answer: at 900 characters a minute — the basis every figure below uses — the same allowance buys 11.1% more finished minutes than that column claims.

Read the last two columns together. Every paid block on this ladder costs about $1 per 12,500 characters ($0.08 per 1,000), so there is no volume discount to wait for — but the blocks are fixed, and you cannot buy a top-up. A workload that lands just over a block boundary buys the next whole block: 600 minutes needs 540,000 characters, which is $43.20 of arithmetic and a $99 plan. That is the only real pricing decision on the page, and the plan table does not show it.

The cliff is wide enough to change what you write. Creator's allowance stops at 275,000 characters, which is 305.6 finished minutes at 900 characters a minute; the next block does not stop until 1,375.6. So minute 307 costs $77 on its own — the same audio, one minute longer, 4.5x the bill. If your month lands in that band, either use the headroom or trim the script: a few characters off the boundary is the only discount on this ladder.

Price it for your own script

Characters in your script are the input — no estimate needed. If your finished audio is 43 minutes, paste the script into a character counter, take that number, and read it against the allowance column above. If you only know the duration, multiply minutes by 900 for the table above, or by 1,000 if you want the vendor's own column.
