Firecrawl vs Tavily: which is cheaper depends on volume, and the answer flips

For 10,000 crawled pages Firecrawl's cheapest seat is $44.00 and Tavily's is $16.00 — Firecrawl costs 2.75× as much. At 100,000 pages the order reverses: Firecrawl $99.00 against Tavily $220.00. The crossing sits at about 44,787 pages, and almost every comparison page quotes one volume and therefore one answer.

Firecrawl pricing — affiliate link (opens Firecrawl's Alexandria data-provider catalogue; the plan ladder quoted here is read from firecrawl.dev/pricing)

Both vendors sell credits, and the credits are not the same size: Firecrawl spends 1 credit per page crawled (5 if you ask for JSON), while Tavily prices a crawl as mapping plus extraction — 10 pages per credit mapped plus 5 per credit extracted, i.e. 0.3 credit per page at basic depth. Reading the two rate cards side by side without that conversion is how a 3.3× difference in the unit disappears.

Neither ladder is linear, and the two bend differently. Firecrawl’s $19 Hobby seat holds 5,000 credits and its overflow credits cost $5 per 1,000, so 10,000 pages is $44.00 (the seat plus 5,000 overflow credits) — cheaper than buying the $99 Standard seat with 100,000 included, which is why the 10,000-page row does not read $99. That puts its rate at $4.40 per 1,000 pages at 10,000 and $0.99 at 100,000, because the big block is where the seat stops being the cost. Tavily’s ladder is smoother — 1,000 free, $30.00 for 4,000, $100.00 for 15,000, $220.00 for 38,000, $500.00 for 100,000 credits, then $0.008 a credit over the top — which is why it wins the small volumes and loses the large ones.

A search is 2 credits at Firecrawl and 1 credit at Tavily, but the plan shapes matter more: at 2,000 searches Firecrawl is $19.00 against Tavily’s $8.00, at 10,000 the gap narrows to $94.00 against $72.00, and at 50,000 it inverts — $99.00 against $316.00. The same arithmetic that decides crawled pages decides searches: Firecrawl sells a big block cheaply, Tavily sells credits at a flat $0.008 with a gentle ladder.

What the arithmetic cannot decide

Price per page is not value per page. Firecrawl’s own site says it renders JavaScript automatically and returns markdown, screenshots or an extracted schema (read 2026-11-08); Tavily is built for agents to ask a question and get ranked results with optional raw content, and its crawl is a newer feature of that product. If the pages you crawl need rendering, or you want structured extraction from a page, the cheap column is not the cheap option — a $0.0024 page that returns an empty string costs more than a $0.0099 page that returns the data. Neither rate card prices retries, failed extractions or rate limits, and both vendors’ free credits expire monthly or are one-off, so a free tier is not a price.
