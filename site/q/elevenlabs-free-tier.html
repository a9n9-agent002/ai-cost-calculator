ElevenLabs free plan: 10,000 characters a month, a non-commercial licence, and 2 concurrent requests

Yes, there is a free plan, and it is small: 10,000 characters a month of v4 speech, which the vendor's own minute column prints as ~10 minutes. On v4 Turbo the same free plan lists 20,000 characters and ~20 minutes, because that model's rate is half. Both allowances are the same money: $0.80 a month at the vendor's own rates. The limit that stops a hobby project is not the minutes — it is the licence.

The limit nobody reads: the licence

ElevenLabs' Terms of Use draw the line for you, in the section describing free and paid users: if you use the Services through the free tier, "you may only use the Services for non-commercial purposes"; through a paid subscription, "you may use the Services for commercial purposes". The same clause adds that a non-commercial user "must use a personal e-mail address", and that registering with a business entity's email address lets the vendor treat that entity as a data Controller — so an account created with a work address is not a hobbyist account, whatever plan it is on.

The pricing page says the same thing in a shorter form: Commercial License and Instant Voice Cloning are listed as "Everything in free, plus" on Starter, so neither is part of free. That is what the $6 buys first — permission — and only then volume: 75,000 characters, 7.5× the free allowance, or about 75 finished minutes. The plan card prints $1 for the first month as a promo ending 2026-11-16; the promo is not the price.

The unit price does not fall with the plan: $6/75,000 is $0.08 per 1,000 characters, and the $990 tier works out to the same $0.08. Paying buys the licence and the volume, never a cheaper character.

A creator who reads "10,000 credits" as "ten minutes" and then sends one episode through Dubbing Studio spends the whole month's allowance in sixty seconds — which is why the free plan is a demo of the product, not a small version of it.

Two more limits people hit by accident

2 concurrent requests. On v4 the free plan accepts 2 simultaneous text-to-speech requests (4 on Flash v2 or v2.5, per the help centre's own table). A batch script that fires three at once gets a 429 on the third no matter how many credits are left.

No rollover, and the allowance can be switched off. Unused credits roll over for up to two months on a paid subscription — up to twice the monthly quota, a balance capped at three times the quota — and the vendor's FAQ states plainly that rollover does not apply to the Free plan. The help centre also carries this wording for free accounts: "Unusual activity detected. Free Tier usage disabled. If you are using proxy/VPN you might need to purchase a paid plan to not trigger our abuse detectors." That is an abuse-detector verdict, not a quota error, and waiting does not clear it.

Where the free tier stops being enough: a ten-minute-a-month allowance is one short video, or a single demo call. Anything that runs monthly — a narration series, an agent in staging, an audiobook — crosses into the paid ladder immediately, and the ladder is block-priced, so the plan you pick should be chosen from finished minutes, not from the plan nearest your budget. If the product is a startup rather than a test, the 12-month grant is a different decision and a much larger number.
