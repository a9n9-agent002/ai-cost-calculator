DigitalOcean droplet bandwidth pricing: 1,000 GiB included, then $0.01 a GiB — and the allowance accrues by the second

The $6 Droplet's transfer allowance is real and it is not the number people think: 1,000 GiB of outbound transfer a month, after which DigitalOcean bills $0.01 per GiB. Serve 2 TiB and the $6 Droplet costs $16.48 that month; the bandwidth line is the larger of the two. Inbound transfer is free.

The included transfer is worth more per GiB than the overage costs. A $6 Droplet's 1,000 GiB work out to $0.006 a GiB inside the plan, against $0.01 a GiB for anything past it — so the allowance is worth buying a plan for, and the shape of the bill is "cheap until you cross the line, then 1.7× dearer". Serving 1,600 GiB in a month doubles a $6 Droplet's bill.

The part the price page does not say: the allowance accrues per second

From the same billing page: "For every second a Droplet exists it accrues 1/2,419,200 of its plan's transfer allowance towards the team pool, capped at 2,419,200 seconds (28 days) per monthly billing cycle." The denominator is 2,419,200 seconds, which is 28 days — so a Droplet that exists for one week has accrued 25% of its monthly allowance, not all of it. A $6 Droplet alive for those 7 days can therefore send 250 GiB before the meter starts; a 500 GiB burst in that week costs $2.50 in overage on a plan the marketing copy calls "1,000 GiB included". Short-lived Droplets, blue-green deploys and CI runners are exactly the case where the marketing number and the accrued number differ.

What does not count

  • Inbound transfer to Droplets is free.
  • Data transfer between Droplets over the VPC private network is not billed (it uses a private interface).
  • Outbound transfer that DigitalOcean determines was dropped by a DigitalOcean firewall rule is not billed.
  • All IPv6 traffic uses the public interface, so it counts against the pool.

The VPC line matters most for anyone running more than one Droplet: app-to-database traffic over the private interface is not billed, while the same bytes over public addresses would be. A firewall rule that drops traffic also drops the charge — the page says outbound transfer "that we determine is dropped by a DigitalOcean firewall rule" is not billed.

The team pool, and what it does not do

Transfer is pooled across every Droplet on the team: the allowance of a mostly idle Droplet covers the overage of a busy one. DigitalOcean's own example: "If one Droplet uses 1,500 GiB and another 100 GiB against a 2,000 GiB team pool, they cumulatively used 1,600 GiB, so no overage fees apply." The limits are that the pool does not roll over between months and cannot move between teams, so a quiet month cannot bank transfer for a launch month.

How to see your own number

The billing page has a Droplet Transfer (Bandwidth) Overview with a projection based on your daily averages, and a Bandwidth Detail CSV (Beta) export. Read it before you resize: the projection assumes your current daily average holds to the end of the cycle, which is exactly the assumption a launch month breaks.
