Cheapest line is not cheapest workload

At 40M input and 10M output tokens a month, the bill runs from $1.30 (Llama 3.1 8B Instruct) to $360.00 (Kimi K3.1 Pro Highspeed) — 276.9× for the same token count, because a model with a cheap input rate and an expensive output rate is the wrong choice for a chat workload, and vice versa for summarisation.

The 1 $0 listing is excluded from both numbers on purpose: a free model is usually a different model with different limits, not the paid one at a discount.

What it costs to walk in the door: nothing for the Sandbox, $10.01 for the models

Novita's own billing docs give the number its marketing page does not. Manual top-ups "must be greater than $10" (Payment Methods, last modified 2026-11-05), payment is Stripe card or debit only, and the FAQ says refunds are "generally not available". For token inference and GPU instances, no page on novita.ai states the size of its sign-up voucher — the FAQ mentions one only in the sentence where the system revokes it, "when suspicious registration activity is detected" — so the only figure a buyer can plan on is the floor: $10.01, read 2026-11-09T03:12Z.

At the rates in the table above, that floor buys 385.0M tokens of the cheapest 4:1 chat mix on the page (Llama 3.1 8B Instruct — $0.02 in, $0.05 out) and only 1.4M of the dearest (Kimi K3.1 Pro Highspeed — $4 in, $20 out), a 277× spread in what the same minimum payment buys. Or 5.9 hours of the cheapest H100 spot hour at $1.70. Neither is a free tier, and none of it is refundable. Against the other three programs priced on this site: Firecrawl's free plan is 1,000 credits a month with no card (500 searches or 1,000 pages), ElevenLabs gives $0.80 of characters a month on a non-commercial licence and sells its first paid month for $1, and DigitalOcean's signup credit is $5 for 90 days (its docs, read 2026-11-09). For inference, Novita is the one where the first dollar is a commitment.

There is a no-card entry — but it is the Sandbox, not the models

The Sandbox pricing page (last modified 2026-11-05, read 2026-11-09T03:21Z) says: "New users can complete a quick account setup survey to receive $100 in promotional credits for eligible Novita Sandbox usage. No credit card is required." The same page scopes it twice: the credits apply to "eligible Novita Sandbox usage" — CPU, RAM and billable storage — and are "subject to availability, validity period, usage scope, and quota limits". Model APIs and GPU instances are not mentioned, so this is not a $100 LLM credit and this page does not present it as one.

Priced against the Sandbox's own per-second rates (1 vCPU $0.0000098/s, 1 GiB $0.0000032/s), $100 is 2,137 hours of a 1 vCPU / 1 GiB sandbox ($0.0468/h) or 429 hours of a 4 vCPU / 8 GiB sandbox ($0.2333/h), running continuously — plus 20 GB of ephemeral storage free per running sandbox and 60 GB of persistent storage free per account. That is the useful form of the offer: an agent runtime you can benchmark for days without a card, not a discount on the model prices in the table above. Credits do not change those unit prices, so the same $100 buys no inference at all.

The same balance is the floor on mistakes

Novita bills a request as soon as it reaches the model: 400, 401, 403, 429 and 5xx responses are free, but a 499 "client disconnected" is charged for the tokens already generated (LLM API Billing). A client timeout at 30 seconds is therefore a paid request, not a retry, and the vendor's own advice is to set max_tokens and a client timeout of at least 60 seconds. Out of a non-refundable balance, an agent that retries on timeout spends twice and is billed for both attempts.
