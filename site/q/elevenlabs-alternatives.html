ElevenLabs alternatives priced per 1,000 characters

If you are pricing a replacement for ElevenLabs, the number that decides it is not the plan price. At 100,000 characters a month — about 100 finished minutes at 1,000 characters a minute — ElevenLabs bills $22.00, because the smallest plan that covers that volume is Creator and its 275,000 characters cannot be bought smaller. OpenAI's tts-1 bills $1.50 for the same characters, Google's Chirp 3 HD $3.00, Polly's Neural voices $1.60 and Cartesia's Pro plan $5.00.

That is a 14.7× spread at this volume, and it comes from two different shapes of pricing rather than from a discount: the three API vendors charge per character with no minimum, while ElevenLabs sells blocks. Pay-as-you-go rates tell you the marginal price; the block tells you the bill.

When ElevenLabs is still the right answer

This table prices characters. It does not price voice cloning, dubbing, the multilingual coverage the model advertises, or the agent product, and several of these vendors simply do not sell those. If your job needs an instant clone of one narrator's voice, or a managed agent that answers a phone, the cheaper rows above are not substitutes — they are a different product. The honest reading of the table is narrower: for plain synthesis of text you already have, at these volumes, ElevenLabs is the most expensive of the five. ElevenLabs pricing — affiliate link (the vendor this page earns from, and the dearest row above) appears here because the product line, not the price, is the reason to use it.

The characters-per-minute figure these tables divide by is a convention, not a measurement: two vendors' own worked examples put a finished minute at 714–780 characters (median 741), so dividing by 1,000 understates what a plan buys and overstates the price of a finished minute. ElevenLabs cost per minute prices that band from the measured rows.
