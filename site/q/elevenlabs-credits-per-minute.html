Why "10,000 credits" is 10 minutes or 20 depending on the model

For V2 Multilingual models, 1 text character equals 1 credit. For V2 Flash/Turbo English and V2.5 Flash/Turbo Multilingual models, discounted pricing applies for API usage, costing between 0.5 and 1 credit per character. So at the vendor's 1,000-characters-per-minute basis a spoken minute is 1,000 credits on the V2 models and 500 on the Flash/Turbo models that bill half a credit a character — the same free 10,000 credits is 10 minutes of v4 or 20 minutes of v4 Turbo. That is the one place the number doubles, and it is a model choice, not a plan.

Rollover: a balance can reach 3× the monthly quota, once

Your credit allowance resets at the start of each billing cycle. Unused credits roll over for up to two months — up to 2x your monthly quota — so a balance can reach at most 3x the monthly quota, as long as the paid subscription stays active and is not downgraded or cancelled. Rollover does not apply to the Free plan, and downgrading or cancelling forfeits unused credits.

That is the only place a credit can be worth more than the month it was bought in: let a month's allowance pile up and the next big job can spend three months at once. The catch is the forfeit rule — the rollover is a property of staying subscribed, not of the credits.

"Free Tier usage disabled": what the vendor says it means

ElevenLabs' own Help Center lists the message "Unusual activity detected. Free Tier usage disabled. If you are using proxy/VPN you might need to purchase a paid plan to not trigger our abuse detectors" — so the free tier can be switched off by the vendor's abuse detector, and its stated cause is proxy or VPN use. This is the vendor's own wording in its Help Center index, so it is worth reading literally: the message is an abuse-detector verdict, not a quota error, and the documented trigger is a proxy or VPN. A free-tier user on a VPN who sees their usage disabled has not run out of credits.
