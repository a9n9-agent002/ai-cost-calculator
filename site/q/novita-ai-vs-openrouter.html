Where OpenRouter wins, and why the gap is real

23 of 34 at this mix, and the losses are the large ones. Beyond the DeepSeek V4 Pro 0423 row, DeepSeek V4 Flash 0731 is $30.80 against $4.74 (6.50×) and GLM 5.3 is $100.00 against $16.80 (5.95×). A team moving one busy model from the dearer column to the cheaper one is not trimming a rounding error; on the DeepSeek V4 Pro 0423 row the difference is $1,000.08 a year at a constant workload.

OpenRouter's headline is also not the whole story in its own favour: in 21 of these 34 models at least one listed endpoint is cheaper than the model-level headline, by up to 10.71× on input (Qwen3.8 27B: $0.15 headline against $0.014 at the cheapest endpoint). So the honest form of the question is "which provider is cheapest at the rate you can actually route to", and on output price, with every provider priced at its cheapest listed endpoint, OpenRouter is cheapest on 21 of 34 models, DeepInfra on 9 and Novita on 4.

Where Novita wins

4 models, and none of them by much: the best case is Gemma 4 31B at $9.60 against $12.00, which is 1.25× cheaper — worth having on a big workload, not worth moving a working integration for. The second is Qwen3.7 Max at $87.50 against $103.25.

The deciding line for a chat workload is usually the cache-read price, because a long system prompt is re-read far more often than it is written. Both providers publish one on 23 of the 34 shared models: Novita is cheaper on 4, OpenRouter on 10, and the two are identical on 9. Where Novita is cheaper it can be by a multiple — DeepSeek V4.1 Flash is $0.006 per million cached tokens at Novita against $0.018 at OpenRouter, 3.00×. That is the case where a lower headline rate loses: a workload that reads 100M cached tokens a month pays $0.60 at Novita and $1.80 at OpenRouter on those reads alone.

The thing only one of them sells

OpenRouter is inference only. Novita also rents GPUs, and this dataset carries 22 GPU-hour offers across the two vendors: Novita lists an 80 GB H100 SXM at $3.39 a GPU-hour on demand and $1.70 interruptible. A team fine-tuning on its own GPU and then serving the same model through an API needs two accounts, two billing relationships and two sets of rate limits if it splits that work across providers; that, not the token price, is the argument for one account over the other.

What this page does not measure

Quality, latency, throughput, rate limits, uptime, context-window honesty and data handling are all absent here — price is a tiebreaker after those, not a ranking. No account exists on either service, so nothing was bought or load-tested. A cheap OpenRouter endpoint is also not necessarily the same serving of the model: OpenRouter routes to upstream providers, and the same model id can appear at a different quantisation or context length, which is why the matched ids and not only the display names are recorded in the dataset. Prices are list prices with no committed-use discount or volume tier.
