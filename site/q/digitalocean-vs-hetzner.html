DigitalOcean vs Hetzner: what the same server costs at 730 hours

Two clouds, one footprint: 2 vCPU and 4 GiB. DigitalOcean lists $24.00 a month for it. Hetzner lists €5.99, which is $6.69 at the ECB rate of 2026-11-06 — 3.59× less for the same cores and memory, with 20 TB of EU traffic against DigitalOcean's 4 TB on that plan. Now the same table one row down: at 1 vCPU and 1 GiB Hetzner is the most expensive of the three, €6.49 ($7.25) against DigitalOcean's $6.00 and Vultr's $5.00. "Hetzner is cheaper" is true at 4 GiB and false at 1 GiB, and a comparison that picks one size can say either.

One row of that table is worth reading twice. Hetzner's own page sells 4 GiB and 2 vCPU for €5.99 in its cost-optimised line and 1 GiB and 1 vCPU for €6.49 in its regular-performance line — 4× the RAM for 8% less money. The cheap line is cheap because it runs proven older hardware, and the smallest plan sits on the new one, so size does not order this price list. VAT is excluded too, so the euro figure is not the amount an EU customer is charged.

The monthly cap is part of the price, and the three caps are not equally generous

Each vendor sells an hourly rate and a monthly price, and the monthly price is a ceiling: run the server all month and you pay the ceiling, not the hourly rate times 730 hours. The gap between those two numbers is where the caps differ. DigitalOcean's $6.00 equals 671.9 hours at its own $0.00893/hour, so a 730-hour month would cost 8.6% more than the cap were the cap not applied. Hetzner's €6.49 equals only 624.0 hours at €0.0104/hour — the cap absorbs 17.0%, its pricing page says so in as many words ("your server's bill will never exceed its monthly price cap"). Vultr's $5.00 equals 714.3 hours at $0.007/hour, a cap worth 2.2%.

The exception is the one Vultr advertises: its VX1 line is "billed on actual hours used each month and are not capped at 672 hours", which puts the number that DigitalOcean and Hetzner hide into Vultr's own copy. It matters when the line has no cap at all: VX1's 2 vCPU / 8 GiB is $0.06/hour, so 730 hours is $43.80 — 4.36× Hetzner's €8.99 ($10.04) 4 vCPU / 8 GiB plan. The specs are not like-for-like — VX1 sells dedicated vCPUs, Hetzner's CX33 shared ones — but the hourly rate is the number people compare, and here it is 4.36× apart at month end.

Traffic is the line item that can outweigh compute

At 2 vCPU / 4 GiB, DigitalOcean includes 4,000 GiB of transfer, Vultr 3 TB and Hetzner 20 TB in its EU locations. A service pushing 30 TB a month through a $24 Droplet is 26 TB over an allowance that a Hetzner EU server does not charge for at all. Two honest caveats: Hetzner's own page puts the Singapore allowance at 0.5 TB with a per-TB charge for additional traffic, so the 20 TB is a European number; and transfer allowances are not throughput, so this says nothing about how fast any of the three moves bytes.

What this comparison cannot tell you

It cannot tell you which is faster. Shared vCPU plans of the same core count are not the same machine: CPU generations, steal time and disk throughput differ, and a plan that is 3× cheaper per month is 3× cheaper because the cores are shared more aggressively. It also cannot tell you what you will pay: Hetzner's prices here are excluded from VAT and billed in euros, so an EU customer pays more than the figure in the table and a non-EU customer's card is charged at the rate of the day, not the 2026-11-06 rate used here. Vultr's prices vary by location and it says so. Availability and stock also move — the pages mark plans as unavailable while retaining their published price, so a plan in this table is a price, not a promise that you can buy it today. And none of this covers managed databases, block storage, snapshots or support plans, which is where a cheap server stops being a cheap stack.
