The $0.075 against $0.072 headline is not a like-for-like comparison

The number most often quoted for Deepgram is $0.075 a minute, and the number most often quoted for ElevenLabs is $0.072 a minute (0.080 per 1,000 characters at 900 characters per spoken minute). Put side by side they differ by 4.2% — but they are not the same product. $0.075 is Deepgram's Voice Agent: speech-to-text, a managed LLM and speech synthesis in one minute, with no separate model bill. $0.072 is ElevenLabs synthesis alone; the text has to arrive from somewhere and the LLM is not in it.

Agent against agent they are within a few percent: ElevenLabs’ $0.08 is 6.7% above Deepgram’s $0.075 pay-as-you-go and 17.6% above its $0.068 Growth rate — and Deepgram’s minute already includes a managed LLM while ElevenLabs’ does not, so all-in the gap is wider than the sticker shows. The layer where the two are furthest apart is the one the headline never mentions: Deepgram’s speech-to-text is $0.0036–$0.0092 a minute, which is 8.2× to 20.8× cheaper than its own agent minute. Reading the agent rate as a transcription rate is a 21× error at the floor, and it is the most common one in this comparison.

Inside ElevenLabs, the model choice moves the price more than the vendor choice

ElevenLabs Multilingual v4 is $0.08 per 1,000 characters and Turbo Flash is $0.04 — exactly half. At 900 characters a minute that is $0.036 against $0.072 for a minute of speech. A page that says “ElevenLabs is 5.3× Deepgram” has compared v4 with Aura-1; on Turbo Flash the same comparison is 2.67×, and against Aura-2 it is 1.33×. Which voice you need is not a price question — it is the one thing this page cannot price, and it can easily be worth more than a 2× rate difference.

The block boundary, which is the other way this bill surprises people

ElevenLabs is sold in monthly blocks, and a block is not a rate. 10,000 finished minutes at 900 characters is 9,000,000 characters; at the vendor’s own $0.08 per 1,000 characters that is $720.00 of usage. The smallest plan that includes 9,000,000 characters is Business at $990.00 a month, which carries 12,375,000 characters — so the month pays for 3,375,000 characters it does not speak, and the effective rate is $0.10 a finished minute instead of $0.072. Blocks are why two months with visibly different usage can produce the same invoice.

What this page cannot tell you

It cannot tell you which voice sounds better, which is the actual buying decision, and it cannot price latency, concurrency limits, language coverage or word-error rate — the numbers that decide whether a transcription bill is cheap or merely small. It also cannot price across ElevenLabs’ two products: its API page includes 275,000 characters on the $22.00 Creator plan — exactly $0.08 per 1,000, the pay-as-you-go rate, because the ladder is flat — while its Creative plans page advertises 121,000 credits for the same $22. Credits are not characters: read the credits number as characters and you get $0.1818 per 1,000, 2.27× the API rate, which is a units error and not a price. All prices here are list prices read on the dates on this page, not invoices, and neither vendor’s free-tier credit is counted as a price.
